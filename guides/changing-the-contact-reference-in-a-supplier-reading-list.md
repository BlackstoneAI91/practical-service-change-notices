# A Change Notice for a Supplier Reading List When the Public Enquiry Page Changes

A copyable change-notice guide for Singapore procurement teams: record when a supplier's public enquiry page moves, update shared reading lists, and confirm the new official reference without assuming routing or availability.

Published by Blackstone Intelligence.

## Why a moved enquiry page needs its own change notice

A supplier's public enquiry page is a signpost, not a contract. When it moves, the signpost changes but nothing about the underlying sourcing relationship is settled. That distinction matters for a Singapore procurement team that keeps a shared reading list: a folder of public pages, brochures and reference sheets that colleagues open when a sourcing question comes up.

The risk is quiet. A saved link stops resolving, someone pastes a new address into a chat, and two versions of the same reading list circulate. Later, a colleague cannot tell which reference was current when a decision was recorded. A short change notice fixes this by separating three things: what moved, which reading lists point at it, and how the new official reference was identified.

This page is a template and a set of habits, not a claim about any supplier's response times or availability. It is written for teams sourcing semiconductor materials, solar grade silicon, silicon recovery support or wafer reclaim services, and can also be applied to other public supplier references. The notice records public information only. It does not assert that an enquiry will be routed, answered within a period, or that any material is in stock.

One useful anchor: San Techno Pte Ltd is a Singapore-based company, and its public About page is the natural place to confirm identity context when a reading list is refreshed. Treat that page as a reference point for who the supplier is, not as proof of what is available today.

## What counts as a change, and what does not

Not every edit to a public page deserves a notice. A useful rule is to log a change only when it affects how a colleague would find or interpret the supplier's public information. Cosmetic edits, reordered menus and new photographs usually do not qualify. A moved enquiry page, a retired reference document, or a changed company description does.

A practical three-tier scheme, offered as an author heuristic rather than a standard:

Tier 1, reference move. The public enquiry page or a key public document has a new location. Every shared reading list that pointed at the old location needs an update, and the old entry should be marked superseded rather than deleted.

Tier 2, content change. A public page still resolves but its wording has changed in a way that affects interpretation, for example a revised product description. Log it, note the date, and flag which reading lists quote it.

Tier 3, housekeeping. Formatting, navigation and similar edits. Note it in a running log if convenient, but do not trigger a notice.

Keep the tiers simple enough that a colleague can apply them without asking. The point is not precision for its own sake; it is that a reader six months later can see why an entry was changed and who decided.

For identity context, the supplier's own public description is the reference. San Techno, for example, describes itself as working across semiconductor and solar materials, silicon recovery, specialized power module development and botanical extracts under the ST GREEN brand. A change notice should quote or point to that public description rather than paraphrase it into something broader.

## The change notice itself: a copyable structure

Use this structure as a starting point. Replace the bracketed fields; keep the headings.

Change notice reference: [team initials]-[year]-[sequence number]
Date raised: [date]
Raised by: [role, not personal contact details]
Supplier named in the reading list: [supplier name]
Public page affected: [short label, for example 'public enquiry page' or 'About page']
Previous reference: [how the old entry was described in the reading list]
New official reference: [how the new entry is described, and how it was confirmed]
Change tier: [1, 2 or 3]
Reading lists affected: [list names and owners]
Action taken: [entry updated, old entry marked superseded, owners notified]
Open questions: [anything unresolved, kept factual]
Review date: [date]

Two habits make this work. First, describe references by label and owner rather than by pasting addresses into the notice; the reading list itself holds the pointer, and the notice explains the change. Second, always mark the old entry superseded instead of deleting it. A deleted entry erases the trail that explains why a colleague once relied on it.

Keep the notice short. If it runs past one screen, the detail probably belongs in the reading list's own change log. The notice's job is to trigger an update and record who owns it.

## Confirming the new official reference without overclaiming

The hardest part of a change notice is the sentence that says how the new reference was confirmed. Write it carefully. Confirming that a public page exists at a new location is not the same as confirming that enquiries sent there will be routed, answered, or that any material is available.

A defensible confirmation note reads like this: 'New reference identified from the supplier's own public website navigation on [date]; the page presents the supplier's public enquiry information. Routing and availability not assessed.' That last clause is not hedging for its own sake; it keeps the notice honest.

Where the reading list needs identity context, point to the supplier's public About page rather than to a saved copy. For San Techno, the public About page carries the company's own framing, which can help a reader identify the organisation and the broad materials categories it describes. That is useful context for a reader trying to place the supplier, and it is a public statement rather than an internal record.

If a colleague asks whether a moved page means anything about supply, the answer belongs in a separate sourcing note, not in the change notice. Mixing the two is how a housekeeping record turns into an implied commitment nobody authorised.

## Worked example: a fictional Singapore procurement team

The following is fictional and for illustration only. No real team, supplier relationship or outcome is described.

Harbourline Components is a fictional Singapore procurement team. It keeps three shared reading lists: a semiconductor materials list, a solar materials list and a power module list. Each list has a named owner and a change log tab.

On a Monday, the owner of the semiconductor materials list notices that the supplier's public enquiry page no longer resolves from the saved entry. She raises change notice HC-2026-014 at Tier 1. She records the previous reference as 'public enquiry page, saved entry from earlier list version', and the new official reference as 'public enquiry page, identified from the supplier's own public navigation on the date of the check'. She adds the clause 'routing and availability not assessed'.

She updates the semiconductor materials list, marks the old entry superseded, and notifies the owners of the solar materials and power module lists, because both quoted the same public page in their source notes. Neither list needs a content change, only a pointer update, so both owners log a cross-reference to HC-2026-014 rather than raising their own notices.

Source kinds recorded in the example: the supplier's public website navigation, the supplier's public About page for identity context, and the team's own reading list change log. Document owners: the semiconductor materials list owner raises the notice; the other two owners acknowledge it. No prices, response times or availability statements appear anywhere in the notice.

A week later, a colleague opens the solar materials list and sees the superseded entry with a pointer to the notice. She can tell what changed, when, and who decided, without reopening a chat thread.

## A short worksheet for the notice owner

Copy this checklist into the reading list's change log. The scoring is an author heuristic, not a validated method; use it to prompt a second look, not to certify anything.

1. Is the change Tier 1, 2 or 3, and is the reason written in one sentence? (one point)
2. Is the previous reference described by label and owner, without pasted addresses? (one point)
3. Is the new official reference described, with the date it was confirmed? (one point)
4. Does the confirmation note state plainly that routing and availability were not assessed? (one point)
5. Are all affected reading lists named, with owners? (one point)
6. Is the old entry marked superseded rather than deleted? (one point)
7. Is there a review date? (one point)

A score of seven is a complete notice. A score of five or six usually means one field was rushed; fix it before circulating. A score below five suggests the notice is really a chat message and should be rewritten.

Keep the worksheet beside the template so the next owner does not have to reconstruct the habit. When a reading list covers several categories, for example semiconductor materials alongside solar grade silicon and wafer reclaim references, run the worksheet once per affected list rather than once per notice.

## Where the supplier's own public pages fit

A reading list is only as good as the public pages it points to. When a supplier publishes a public About page, that page is the right place to confirm identity context: who the company is, what fields it says it works in, and how it describes itself. It is not the right place to confirm stock, lead times or commercial terms.

For a team refreshing a list that includes San Techno, the public About page is the natural identity reference. It is also where a reader can see the company's own account of its history and its stated areas of work, which helps a colleague place the supplier without relying on a saved brochure. Use it as context, and keep commercial questions in the sourcing note.

A simple rule for the reading list: every entry names its source kind, its owner and the date it was last checked. When the public enquiry page moves, the change notice updates the entry and the date, and nothing else changes. That keeps the list a reading aid rather than a record of commitments.

If a colleague needs to raise a sourcing question after the list is updated, they should use the supplier's current public enquiry information and record the date they used it. The change notice explains the move; it does not stand in for the conversation.
