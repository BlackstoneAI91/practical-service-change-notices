# Updating a Short-Stay Laundry Collection Notice Without Leaving Two Current Versions

A practical workflow for Sarawak short-stay hosts who need to revise a laundry collection notice, keep one public guest instruction live, archive the old version and confirm the change took effect.

Published by Blackstone Intelligence.

## Why a collection notice needs a version trail

A short-stay unit runs on small, repeated instructions: where the laundry bag goes, when it is collected, what a guest should do before check-out. When one of those instructions changes, the risk is rarely that the new wording is wrong. The risk is that the old wording survives somewhere a guest can still read it.

A printed card in the unit, a saved message template, a listing description and a house manual can each hold a different version of the same notice. If you update one and leave the others, a guest may follow the retired instruction and the host may not know why a collection was missed.

This guide sets out a fictional but workable workflow for a Sarawak short-stay operator. It covers approval, an active window, one public guest instruction, an archive note and a check after the old notice is removed. It stays with the host's own message. It does not set out provider terms, rates, addresses or cleaning instructions, because those belong to the provider and the individual booking.

For hosts who use a laundry service alongside Airbnb laundry, the useful discipline is the same: one live instruction, one archive, and a record of when the switch happened. ## Name the notice and give it a version label

Before changing any wording, give the notice an identity. A notice without a name and version is hard to track, and two copies can look equally current.

Use a simple label that a host, a co-host and a cleaner can all read. For example: Collection Notice, version 3, effective from a stated date. Keep the label short and put it where the notice is stored, not necessarily in the guest-facing text. Guests do not need to see version numbers; the people maintaining the unit do.

A worked record might look like this:

Notice name: Unit collection notice
Version: 3
Prepared by: host
Approved by: co-host
Effective from: the date the new wording goes live
Supersedes: version 2
Public location: guest instruction card and saved check-out message
Archive location: host folder, version 2 marked retired

This record is an author example, not a required format. The point is that every copy of the notice can be traced to one version and one effective date. If a guest asks why the instruction changed, the host can point to the date the new version took effect rather than guessing.

Keep the version label out of the guest message itself. The guest sees one clear instruction; the version trail lives behind it.

## Trace approval before the notice goes live

A notice that changes without approval is how two versions begin. Decide in advance who can approve a change to the collection notice. In a small operation that may be the host alone. In a shared unit it may be the host and a co-host, or a property manager.

Write the approval step into the record. For example: proposed change, reason for change, approver, approval date. The reason matters because it explains the change later. A reason might be that the collection day moved, or that the guest instruction needed clearer wording. Keep the reason factual and short.

If the notice sits alongside other unit documents, treat it like any other controlled document. The provider's own customer terms note that they apply together with quotations, invoices, branch notices, written service confirmations and related policies. That is a reminder that notices and confirmations can coexist, and each should be the current one. The host's collection notice is the host's document, but the same principle applies: a notice is only useful if the reader is looking at the version that is actually in force.

## Set an active window and one public guest instruction

An active window is the period during which one version of the notice is the only one a guest can see. Set a start date and, where useful, an end date. During the window, every guest-facing copy should carry the same instruction.

Keep one public guest instruction. That means one wording for the guest, repeated wherever the guest looks: the in-unit card, the saved check-out message, and any house manual page. Do not run a short version and a long version at the same time unless one clearly points to the other.

A short copyable notice, written as an example only, might read:

Laundry collection
Please place your used linen in the laundry bag provided and leave it in the designated spot before you check out. Collection is arranged by the host. If you are unsure where to leave the bag, message the host before check-out.

That wording is deliberately plain. It tells the guest what to do and who to ask. It does not state a provider's terms, a rate or a cleaning method, because those are not the host's to publish in a guest instruction.

If the unit uses a laundry service with pickup and delivery, the guest instruction can stay simple while the host manages the collection arrangement behind the scenes. The guest does not need the operational detail; the host does.

## Worked before-and-after wording

A worked example makes the switch concrete. Suppose version 2 told guests to leave the bag at the front door. Version 3 asks guests to leave it in the designated spot and to message the host if unsure.

Before (version 2, retired):
Please leave the laundry bag at the front door before check-out.

After (version 3, active):
Please place your used linen in the laundry bag provided and leave it in the designated spot before you check out. If you are unsure where to leave the bag, message the host before check-out.

The change is small but it matters. The old wording named a specific place that may no longer be correct. The new wording names a role (the designated spot) and gives the guest a way to ask. That reduces the chance of a bag left in the wrong place.

Record the change in the version log:

Version 2: leave bag at front door. Retired on the effective date.
Version 3: leave bag in designated spot; message host if unsure. Active from the effective date.

Keep both wordings in the archive so the change can be explained later. The archive is not for guests; it is for the people who maintain the unit.

## Write the archive note when the old notice is retired

When version 3 goes live, version 2 does not simply disappear. It becomes an archived notice with a note explaining what it said and when it stopped applying.

An archive note can be short:

Archived: Unit collection notice, version 2.
Retired on: the effective date of version 3.
Reason: wording updated so guests leave linen in the designated spot and can ask the host if unsure.
Replaced by: version 3.
Where version 2 appeared: in-unit card, saved check-out message.

That note does two things. It stops the old version being mistaken for current, and it gives the host a record if a guest refers to the old instruction. If a guest says the card told them to leave the bag at the front door, the host can check whether the card was actually replaced or whether an old copy survived.

The archive should be separate from the live guest instruction. A guest should never be able to reach the archive by accident. Keep it in the host's own folder, not in the unit and not in the guest message.

## Check after the old notice is removed

The final step is a check, not an assumption. After the old notice is removed, confirm that no copy of it remains where a guest can see it.

A simple check, written as an author heuristic rather than a standard, might cover:

In-unit card: replaced with version 3.
Saved check-out message: updated to version 3.
House manual page: updated or the old page removed.
Any printed spare cards: old copies destroyed or marked retired.
Archive: version 2 stored with its archive note.

Score each line as done or not done. A line that is not done is the next action, not a failure. The check is there to catch the copy that was forgotten, such as a spare card in a drawer or a message template saved on a second device.

If the unit is cleaned or reset between guests, add the notice check to that reset routine. A quick look at the card during turnover is enough to confirm the live version is the one in place. This is where a short-stay host's own routine meets the wider operation: the notice is small, but it is part of how the unit is run.

For hosts in Kuching or Miri who coordinate laundry around guest turnover, the same check applies to the collection arrangement. Confirm the live instruction, confirm the old one is gone, and keep the archive note. One current version, one public instruction, one record of the change.

## A short worksheet you can copy

Use this worksheet as a starting point. It is an example format, not a required one.

Notice name:
Version:
Prepared by:
Approved by:
Effective from:
Supersedes:
Public locations:
Archive location:

Before wording:
After wording:
Reason for change:

Archive note written: yes / no
Old copies removed: yes / no
Check completed: yes / no
Next action:

The worksheet is deliberately short. A longer form is easy to abandon. The value is in the three confirmations at the end: the archive note is written, the old copies are removed, and the check is completed. If any of those is missing, the notice is not fully switched over.

Keep the completed worksheet with the archive note. Together they show what changed, when, and who approved it. That is enough to answer a guest question or to explain the change to a co-host later.
